Features

A studio-grade stem splitter, minus the subscription

The same class of AI model as paid online services, running locally, with a real mixer around it.

4 or 6 stems

Vocals, drums, bass and other, or add guitar and piano. Powered by Meta's Hybrid Transformer Demucs.

Built-in mixer

Volume, mute and solo per stem, waveforms, click-to-seek and keyboard control. Every stem stays perfectly in sync.

Tempo and time signature

BPM and meter detected from the separated drums, so you know what to set your metronome or DAW to.

Private by design

The AI runs on your computer. No upload, no account, no tracking. Once installed, it works offline.

Audio or video in

MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A and OGG, plus MP4, MOV, MKV and WEBM. Stemmer pulls the audio out of videos for you.

WAV export, three ways

Any single stem, all stems as a ZIP, or your own mix as one file, at full quality.

How it works

Drop, split, mix

No settings to learn. A 5-minute song takes about 2–3 minutes on an Apple Silicon Mac.

Drop a song

Drag in any audio or video file, or pick one. Choose 4 stems, or 6 if you want guitar and piano too.

AI splits it

Demucs separates the instruments on your computer's GPU or CPU, then Stemmer detects the tempo and meter.

FAQ

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Stemmer is open source under the MIT license. There's no paid tier, no account and no limits on songs.

Is my music uploaded anywhere?

No. The AI model runs on your own computer, and Stemmer only talks to itself on your machine. After installing, it works with no internet connection.

Why does my computer warn me when I open it?

The app isn't signed with a paid Apple Developer ID or Windows code-signing certificate yet, so macOS and Windows show a one-time warning. The source code and build pipeline are public on GitHub, and each release lists SHA-256 checksums.

How long does a song take?

About 2–3 minutes for a 5-minute song on an Apple Silicon Mac. On Windows the AI runs on the CPU, so expect a few minutes, depending on your processor.

How good is the separation?

Stemmer uses Meta's Hybrid Transformer Demucs (htdemucs), one of the best open models for music source separation. Vocals, drums and bass come out very clean. Guitar and piano are harder and depend on the mix.

Can I use the stems commercially?

The app is free to use for anything, but the music belongs to its rights holders. Use stems from copyrighted songs for personal practice, study and transcription unless you have permission.
